# Privacy, Retention, and Content Policy

## 1. Data minimization

Collect information needed to operate the platform, personalize resource discovery, moderate content, and secure accounts. Avoid collecting sensitive profile data without a defined need. Clearly distinguish public profile information from private account and activity records.

## 2. Visibility

- Default resource visibility: all registered users, subject to explicit audience rules.
- Uploaders may choose supported audiences, such as whole-school or selected users, where implemented.
- School membership is self-reported at launch, not verified.
- Private collections and private browsing history are not visible to other ordinary users.
- Admin access to detailed records is for operational, moderation, security, and support purposes and should be audited.

## 3. Activity records

The admin console should show a detailed but understandable record of uploads, relevant actions, reviewed questions, comments, and other necessary activity. Keep records purpose-limited. The planning decision is to retain activity logs for one year, then delete or aggregate them unless a documented legal, security, or moderation reason requires longer retention.

## 4. Account deletion

A deletion request should:
1. verify the account holder;
2. revoke active sessions;
3. remove personal profile data where appropriate;
4. anonymize approved academic contributions when retention is appropriate;
5. remove private collections and personal associations as defined by policy;
6. record minimal audit evidence;
7. apply the same deletion policy to downstream systems and scheduled exports where feasible;
8. account for backup expiration rather than promising instant erasure from all backup media.

The final policy needs legal review and must comply with applicable law.

## 5. Copyright and permission to share

Users should upload only materials they have permission to share. The platform needs:
- terms of use;
- copyright complaint process;
- repeat-abuse policy;
- takedown/review workflow;
- rules for school-owned or examination materials;
- transparent contributor attribution;
- a process for disputed ownership.

Do not assume that a document being used for education automatically makes redistribution lawful. Obtain appropriate legal advice before public rollout.

## 6. No-answer policy

The initial platform does not accept answers or answer keys and does not generate/publish answers. Explain this at upload time and in contribution rules. If answer material is discovered, route it through the moderation process. Do not promise that automated detection will catch every case.

## 7. Public contribution metrics

Public profile statistics may include permitted counts such as approved contributions and views. Do not expose private browsing trails, private collections, email addresses, or sensitive moderation history. Leaderboards should avoid rewarding spam volume and should emphasize quality and useful corrections.

## 8. Notifications

Allow preferences for routine notifications and email. Urgent school-wide announcements can target all relevant users. Security notices should remain available and not be silently suppressed. Avoid exposing private information in email subject lines or notification previews.

## 9. Retention schedule to formalize

| Data category | Planning direction |
|---|---|
| Original academic files | Long-term retention while permitted and useful; document lifecycle and backup rules |
| Extracted questions | Retain with provenance and contribution policy |
| Activity logs | One year, then delete or aggregate unless documented exception |
| Security/moderation records | Retain as needed under a documented policy |
| Backups | Defined retention, access controls, and expiry |
| Temporary processing files | Delete promptly after processing unless needed for an authorized review |
| Account data | Delete or anonymize according to account-deletion policy |

This is a planning schedule, not a substitute for a legally reviewed privacy policy.
